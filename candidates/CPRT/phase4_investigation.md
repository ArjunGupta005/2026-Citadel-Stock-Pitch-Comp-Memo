## 1. What has management said?
- The insurance volume decline is cyclical: consumers are cutting coverage, collision frequency is down 3.4%, and total-loss frequency is at a record 23.3%. Severity is +8.8%.
- "With the exception of 1 single customer loss domestic insurance assignments would be up 2.3%." Industry reports identify the customer as Progressive. RB Global said on 8/4 that its largest insurance partner is now in all 50 states, with volume "transitioning" over the prior 90 days. Reports put IAA at about 90% of Progressive's units.
- On pricing: "we do not need to play that game". They will not match IAA's price incentives.
- OpEx per car rose 12.7% from new services (long-haul, TitleExpress, wholesale facilities). They gave no cost guidance but say they are "on it".
- **ACV acquisition:** $10.50/share in cash, about $1.9bn, funded from cash, expected to close by the end of calendar 2026. CFO: "breakeven in the current [year] and accretive in the first full year (FY28)".
- **Leadership:** Jay Adair is back as CEO (8-K of 7/8/26). Jane Pocock was named President effective 8/1/26. Jeff Liaw (co-CEO) is no longer on calls.

## 2. What does guidance imply?
There is no formal guidance. The disclosures imply:
- The Progressive loss runs at full rate from Q1-FY27 (Aug-Oct). The comparison does not become easy until about Q4-FY27 (May-Jul 2027).
- After ACV, about $2.6bn of cash remains, versus $4.5bn earning interest. At about 4%, that is roughly $75m a year of lost pre-tax interest income (~$0.06 per share).
- Cost investment continues: "you can extrapolate your expectation with respect to how costs will trend".

## 3. What is the Street expecting?
- Q1-FY27 EPS is $0.397 (-3% y/y), against Q4's -14.6% y/y.
- FY27 EPS is $1.75 (+11% vs $1.58 yr-ago adjusted base), cut from $1.85 30 days ago. Other aggregators show $1.66-1.69.
- Q1-FY27 EBITDA is about $490m (about +4%).
- HSBC downgraded to Hold, calling 2027 "difficult... limited visibility".

## 4. Where is the disagreement?
- **Q1-FY27 has the full Progressive loss, yet the Street has only -3% EPS.** Q4 had a partial (ramping) loss plus a 470bp operating-margin drop and printed -14.6%.
- **The Street's FY27 implies EPS growth in Q2-Q4.** The remaining three quarters must rise about 18% vs FY26 ($1.35 vs $1.14) while the volume loss is still running and interest income falls after ACV.
- **The two-sided element.** Underlying insurance volume ex-customer turned positive (+2.3%), total-loss frequency is at a record, and international is +10%. The bull case is that the cycle is turning just as the lapping begins.

## 5. Which KPI explains it?
**US insurance units y/y**, reported on the Q1-FY27 call (around 11/19/26). Through operating deleverage it drives service revenue and operating margin. Secondary: OpEx per car and operating margin.
