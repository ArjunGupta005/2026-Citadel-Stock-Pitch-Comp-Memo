1. Company Overview

Copart is a global leader in online salvage-vehicle auctions, with 286 operating facilities, more than 4 million vehicles sold annually and approximately 1 million registered members across 185+ countries. Insurers supplied 79% of vehicles processed in FY26. FY26 revenue was $4.67bn, diluted EPS was $1.55, and the company ended the year with a net-cash balance sheet.

Scale, salvage-yard density, buyer liquidity and insurer relationships are meaningful competitive strengths. The weakness exposed by Q4 is customer concentration: one carrier loss was large enough to swing domestic insurance assignment growth by nearly 10 points. Our short is therefore not a quality call; it is a timing call on a discrete FY27 earnings hole. Management execution matters primarily through how quickly costs can be resized to the lower volume base.

2. Industry Overview

Copart competes most directly with RB Global's IAA in a scale-driven salvage-auction market. Salvage auction volumes are driven by vehicle miles traveled, accident frequency, total-loss frequency, repair-cost inflation and used-vehicle values. Total-loss frequency remains structurally supportive: management cited a record second-quarter 23.3% total-loss frequency, with repair costs more than 50% above 2019 levels.

Near term, however, unit mix matters more than the industry backdrop. Copart's Q4 domestic insurance assignments were -7.5% y/y; management said they would have been +2.3% excluding one lost customer, a 9.8-point drag. RB Global said incremental volume from its largest automotive-insurance partner had been transitioning over roughly the prior 90 days as of Aug. 4, following expansion to all 50 states. We treat this as corroboration of a transfer, not proof of carrier identity.

4. Market View

What is already known / widely discussed: the lost customer, near-term margin pressure, an eventual cycle recovery, and the ACV acquisition / near-term integration economics. The stock has already de-rated materially (~18x trailing P/E vs a ~35x 2021-26 median).

What we think is not: consensus still embeds FY27 EPS growth despite the first full-run-rate quarters of the customer loss. Our disagreement is numerical, not narrative.

Why now: Q1 is the first full quarter in which customer-volume loss and cost absorption appear together. Q2 provides a second read and the first GAAP view of ACV (the company guides closing by calendar year-end 2026).

Holding-period discipline: our post-earnings-reaction study (full sample 2004-26, n = 9-22) shows CPRT averaged +4.5% to +6.3% 3-month excess returns after large negative reactions. We therefore target the Q1/Q2 discovery window, not a 12-month structural short.

5. Valuation

We value CPRT at our planned exit, mid-February 2027 (after Q2 FY27). Year-1 earnings are NTM ex-interest EPS (Q3 FY27 + Q4 FY27 + Q1 FY28 + Q2 FY28); year 2 completes the FY28 recovery. Value is the PV of distributions and a terminal value at a 9.74% cost of equity, plus net cash at that date, so the P/E is an output; the year-1 payout is linked to modeled recovery growth (1 - g1/ROIC) and the normalized payout (1 - g/ROIC) applies thereafter. FY28 EPS = standalone CPRT + an explicit ACV contribution (W2 +$0.02, consistent with FY28 accretion guidance).

8. Conclusion

Thesis: Short CPRT through the Q1/Q2 FY27 discovery window: published consensus appears to understate the near-term earnings impact of a large lost insurance customer. Industry: salvage-auction fundamentals remain structurally attractive, but near-term share shifts can overwhelm a supportive total-loss backdrop. Strategy / market view: we are not underwriting permanent impairment; we expect FY27 estimates to reset before normalization in FY28. Valued at our mid-Feb 2027 exit, the probability-weighted value is $24.52 (+10.2% expected short return; W2 $23.10), with a 14.7% W4 loss and a 47.1% rate-cut stress loss. Catalysts: Q1 and Q2 should reveal the full run-rate unit loss, cost absorption, and initial ACV contribution. Risk mitigation: monitor ex-customer unit growth, U.S. margins, OpEx/car, rates and ACV dilution, and exit after Q2 or earlier if our falsification thresholds are breached.
