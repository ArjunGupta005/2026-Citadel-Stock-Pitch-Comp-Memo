 1. Company Overview 
 Copart operates the largest online salvage-vehicle auction platform, with more than 275 locations, more than 4 million vehicles sold annually and approximately 1 million registered members across 185+ countries. Insurers supplied 79% of vehicles processed in FY26. FY26 revenue was $4.67bn , diluted EPS was $1.55 , and the company ended the year with a net-cash balance sheet. 
 Scale, salvage-yard density, buyer liquidity and insurer relationships are meaningful competitive strengths. The weakness exposed by Q4 is customer concentration: one carrier loss was large enough to swing domestic insurance assignment growth by nearly 10 points. Our short is therefore not a quality call; it is a timing call on a discrete FY27 earnings hole. Management execution matters primarily through how quickly costs can be resized to the lower volume base. 
 2. Industry Overview 
 Copart competes most directly with RB Global's IAA in a scale-driven salvage-auction market. Salvage auction volumes are driven by vehicle miles traveled, accident frequency, total-loss frequency, repair-cost inflation and used-vehicle values. Total-loss frequency remains structurally supportive: management cited a record 23.3% in Q2, with repair costs more than 50% above 2019 levels. 
 Near term, however, unit mix matters more than the industry backdrop. Copart's Q4 domestic insurance assignments were -7.5% y/y ; management said they would have been +2.3% excluding one lost customer. RB Global separately disclosed a major automotive-insurance partner expanding to all 50 states and integrating substantial incremental volume across 30 states within 90 days. We treat this as corroboration of a transfer, not proof of carrier identity.

 4. Market View 
 What is already priced in: the customer loss, near-term margin pressure, an eventual cycle recovery, and some ACV dilution are all known. The stock has already de-rated materially. 
 What we think is not: consensus still embeds FY27 EPS growth despite the first full-run-rate quarters of the customer loss. Our disagreement is numerical, not narrative. 
 Why now: Q1 is the first full quarter in which customer-volume loss and cost absorption appear together. Q2 provides a second read and the first meaningful GAAP view of ACV if the transaction closes on schedule. 
 Holding-period discipline: our historical event study shows CPRT often rebounds after large negative earnings reactions. We therefore target the Q1/Q2 discovery window , not a 12-month structural short.

 8. Conclusion 
 Thesis: Short CPRT through the Q1/Q2 FY27 discovery window: published consensus appears to understate the near-term earnings impact of a large lost insurance customer. Industry: salvage-auction fundamentals remain structurally attractive, but near-term share shifts can overwhelm a supportive total-loss backdrop. Strategy / market view: we are not underwriting permanent impairment; we expect FY27 estimates to reset before normalization in FY28. Catalysts: Q1 and Q2 should reveal the full run-rate unit loss, cost absorption, and initial ACV contribution. Risk mitigation: monitor ex-customer unit growth, U.S. margins, OpEx/car, rates and ACV dilution, and exit after Q2 or earlier if our falsification thresholds are breached.
